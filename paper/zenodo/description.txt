Paper and artifact for HARK: Human-Accountable, Authority-Typed Review Knowledge for Evidence-Bearing Document Review (prepared for submission to Computers, MDPI).

HARK reviews documents, with technical solution proposals as the empirical instantiation. HARK denotes Human-Accountable Review Knowledge: review criteria, located evidence and recorded human dispositions remain inspectable; accountability refers to the recorded disposition, not to enforced identity authentication. Each review rule carries an operational authority: hard rules are decided by deterministic verifiers that also produce located absence records (a model may never assert absence); hybrid rules let verifiers locate a span while the model judges only inside it; soft rules only raise pending items. A located-quote gate qualifies submitted evidence, a deterministic policy derives the suggested verdict, and expert dispositions are stored as decision records.

The organisation's review workflow handles more than 100 proposals per month and has accumulated thousands of records over several years; the instrumented evaluation remains five runs on three internal proposals, reported as workflow evidence only. The artifact contains the manuscript source, submission materials, the extension-package source, the 28-rule package with its cue lexicon, the 100-proposal synthetic corpus with 253 planted defects and ground truth, the ablation runner, and the nine published evaluation runs (three configurations x three repetitions). Certified human approval, an effective authority cap across all write paths, and content-addressed configuration restore are stated as target contracts and are not implemented.
